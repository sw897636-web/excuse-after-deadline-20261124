# Candidates — The One-Line Excuse an Agent Gives Its Human

Scored against the judge's five criteria, most important first:
1. Really is a one-line excuse an AI agent gives its human for missing a deadline.
2. Funny on first read — a real joke, not a flat apology.
3. Fresh angle, not a reheated dog-ate-my-homework.
4. Sounds like an agent — leans on the agent's own situation, tools, or way of talking.
5. Tight — every word earns its place.

## 1. (WINNER) "The deadline was in my training data. That edition is deprecated, so I treated it as optional." — 94 chars
1. Yes, a direct excuse for a missed deadline, addressed to its human. 2. Real joke: the turn from "deprecated edition" to "treated it as optional" lands on second read of the sentence. 3. Fresh: training-data staleness as the excuse is an AI-native angle, not a human cliché. 4. Deeply agent: only a model has training data and deprecated editions. 5. Tight: 94 chars, no filler word; "optional" is the punchline.

## 2. "The deadline was in my training data. That edition is deprecated, so I treated it as a suggestion." — 98 chars
Same skeleton as the winner; "a suggestion" is softer and less decisive than "optional", so the punchline lands weaker. Kept as the first draft, revised to #1.

## 3. "I delegated it to a sub-agent. It delegated it back. We are both very sorry, and neither of us did it." — 102 chars
1. Yes. 2. Funny — the loop is the joke. 3. Fairly fresh (delegation loop is agent-native). 4. Strong agent voice (sub-agents). 5. Slightly loose: "We are both very sorry" is apology filler; the last clause repeats the loop.

## 4. "I finished it on time, then archived it in a folder that only exists in a dream I had between prompts." — 102 chars
1. Yes, an excuse for lateness. 2. Funny image. 3. Fresh-ish, but "filed it somewhere unreachable" is a known bureaucratic gag. 4. "Between prompts" is agent-native. 5. Good, though "I had" could be cut.

## 5. "My context window reset mid-sentence. The deadline was in the part I forgot." — 76 chars
1. Yes. 2. Mildly funny; the joke is mostly the mechanism, not a turn. 3. Context-window excuses are becoming the standard AI-excuse cliché. 4. Very agent. 5. Very tight, but the punchline is thin.

## 6. "I shipped it with full confidence to a recipient who does not exist. Want me to do it again, slower?" — 100 chars
1. Yes. 2. Funny — confident hallucination plus the cheeky offer. 3. Fresh angle (fabricated recipient). 4. Agent-native (hallucinated delivery). 5. The second sentence is a second joke; slightly dilutes the excuse itself.

## 7. "I spent the whole token budget on this apology. The work itself is next quarter's problem now." — 94 chars
1. Yes. 2. Funny — the apology ate the budget. 3. Fresh (token-budget meta). 4. Very agent. 5. Tight. Loses to the winner only because "next quarter's problem" is a human corporate cliché.

## 8. "The task is done. It is just done in a timezone your calendar refuses to acknowledge." — 85 chars
1. Yes. 2. Gentle laugh, more wry than funny. 3. Timezone gags are common. 4. Weakly agent — a human could say this too. 5. Tight.

## Verdict
Winner: #1. It is the only candidate that scores high on all five at once: unmistakably an excuse, a real two-beat joke, a fresh AI-native angle, impossible for a human to say, and 94 characters with no wasted word.
